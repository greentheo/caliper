### 2026-11-13 · CALI-177 (#282) · #222 slice C, the make side: bevels, mortises, dado lines, screw marks and glue blocks

The foreman asked for #222 §4 on 2026-11-13. It needs nothing from Theo; slice B waits on his Q2. A new module, `marks.py`, reads each part's own geometry. The patterns, the cut list, the build book, the kids' book and the shopping list all read from it, so they say the same thing.

- **Bevels from the geometry, not from a declared joint.** A sheet edge not square to its faces is found two ways:
  - it lies on another piece's face (a splayed side's top edge under the top);
  - or the solid's own edge face leans (its foot on the floor), read by three rays out through the edge at mid-thickness.

  The angle is the saw's setting, between 2° and 50°. Each bevel carries the cut's line on each face.
  - On the pattern, the outline is the shown face's (rebuilt from its edge lines when the declared face isn't the shown one), the other face's line is dashed, a "B" sits inside the edge, and the page says "saw at 10°, this face up".
  - The cut list says each edge's angle and length; the book says "Set the saw to 10° for the N edges marked B".
- **Mortises and notches** come from `joints._through` (which now also returns its depth span). They sit on the host's pattern as a shaded rectangle the size of the crossing piece, and the page says "cut 3/4 in x 6 in, 10 5/8 in from the left and 2 in up, right through. Size it to your ply's measured thickness". A hole already in the outline isn't marked twice.
- **Dados** are set-in pieces, with their depth taken from the piece's own corners (the finder's samples fall about 2 mm short). A declared rabbet or dado the solid doesn't show is drawn at the proposal's default depth: half the host's thickness for a rabbet, a third for a dado. They're drawn dashed with their depth.
- **Screws:**
  - **Where:** along each edge-on-face joint, 1 1/2 in from each end and then no more than 6 in apart, centred on the edge piece's thickness, crosshairs on the face piece's pattern. They're driven from its far face: a 3/16 in clearance hole, countersunk, and a 1/8 in pilot.
  - **Exceptions:** a through tenon or notch is glued, with no screws. A joint whose `JOINTS` entry gives no screws in its fix has none. A glue block gets two rows, one into each piece.
  - **One count:** the patterns, cut list, book, shopping list (`fasteners.estimate`) and kids' book (`screws_for`, and the marks per pair) all use the marks' count.
  - **Length at a bevel:** checked, and said where a screw would come out of a face (past about 49° at 3/4 in).
- **Glue blocks:** a `1x1` lumber strip (3/4 × 3/4 in) along each joint said to be a glue block. It's weighed in `construction.report`, bought, and on the stick cut list.
  - **Price:** the `1x1` is **priced as a 1x2 ($3.00), the safe side. That price is a guess for the foreman or Theo to settle.**
- **The rule changed for the shopping list:** it was one screw every 6 in, at least two. A 12 in joint now takes 3 screws where it took 2, and a through tenon takes none.
