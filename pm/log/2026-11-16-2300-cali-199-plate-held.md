### 2026-11-16 · CALI-199 (#328) · a panel held over its whole face doesn't span; each piece checked in its own material

Theo's truck camper floor: a 3/8 in ply floor on 2 in XPS walls, a mattress on it, the whole of it standing on the ground.

**The bug, on main (reproduced on a synthetic box):**
- The plate check spanned the floor between the walls as if nothing were under it. The ground was under it.
- It checked the plywood floor at the part's majority material (XPS, by volume), not plywood.
- So the floor read 0.01× while the FEA read 1,361×: a false red that governed the part.
- Buckling had the same fault: each wall in line took the whole backed load at share 1.0.

**The rule (`load_study._backed`, `BACKED = 0.9`):**
- A 7×7 grid over the load's footprint, kept inside the panel's outline, is probed just past the far face. The far face is
  the side the force pushes toward; for a load pulling away from the support, the probe is the near side.
- Points inside a support (grown by 3·tol) are counted. When at least 90% of them are, the panel is backed by the
  support covering most of the footprint.
- The default support ("the floor", whatever the part stands on) counts.
- **A backed panel doesn't span.** plate_check returns `done: False` with `rests_on` and its why: "the load goes
  straight through it into {support}, so it doesn't span: the FEA is the number for it".
- **Buckling:** the walls in line with a backed load take none of it ("doesn't run down this wall"). Walls are dropped
  only for that load, and a load beside it still buckles them.
- **Materials:** `own_material(pc, mat)`. A spanned panel and a buckling wall are checked in their own material; with
  no material named, the part's.

**What a backed-only case reads:**
- The FEA reads None for a backed load (it's blind, #202), so the case can't read ✓.
- The foreman's call (option c, in b's words): it reads unresolved, "Not resolved: the load goes straight through the
  floor into what it stands on: nothing between to check."
- `blind_why(case)` says it in verdicts, answers, release and the page (design.js shows `rests.said`).
- The check that would give it a ✓ (bearing: the wall's crushing across the grain) is filed as **#333**. The value is
  Theo's to set, and #333 isn't in the queue.
