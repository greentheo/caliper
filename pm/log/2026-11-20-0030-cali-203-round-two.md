### 2026-11-20 · CALI-203 (#335) · round two: a thinner sheet thins only its own stock

caliper-reviewer, on #354 at 79cfb35: the rest checked out. But `_thinner` scaled every sheet by (1 − t/t0). On the
MIXED box (2 in XPS walls, a 2 in plywood left end, a 3/4 in plywood floor), the "1½ in" row also shaved the floor to
14.3 mm, a thickness nobody sells: 1.41 lb of its 7.46 lb. Now that the floor weighs as plywood, that's about 16× main's
error.

**The fix** (`lighten._thinner`): a row names one stock, the thickest, and only its sheets thin.
- **Weight:** that stock's sheets, less the step's holes in them, scale by (1 − t/t0). What the step's holes in the other
  stock saved stays saved. For a part of one stock this is exactly the old formula.
- **Safety:** where a check names its piece, a piece of the other stock keeps its own number:
  - a plate check of the floor reads at the floor's own thickness (`_plate_at(..., thick)`);
  - a wall of the other stock buckles at its own.
- **What still scales:** the FEA's number is the part's worst, wherever it is. A sheet that thins may take that over,
  so it scales as before, on the safe side.
- **Theo's designs:** none of his studied parts has two stocks, so nothing of his moves.

**The PR's own test pinned the old formula** (`test_lighten_holes_the_plywood_floor_by_plywood_…`): a thinner row's
savings over every sheet. Changed with the code: the MIXED box's rows, the "1½ in" row among them, now save from the
2 in sheets only. 79cfb35's formula reads exactly the floor's share more.
