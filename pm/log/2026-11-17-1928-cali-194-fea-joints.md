**2. The FEA's joint forces** (`caliper/fea_joints.py`):
- **Read exactly, by nodes.** For each case, each solid voxel's nodal forces are `K u`, using the solver's own scaled
  hex stiffness and each voxel's own multiple. Each voxel is labelled with its piece.
- **Shared nodes.** At a node two pieces share, the whole force is their joint's. Where three or more meet, the
  pairwise forces are the nearest even ones, weighted by each pair's voxel faces there (a small Laplacian solve per
  node). They are equal and opposite, and they give each piece exactly its own nodal force.
- **Each joint's force and moment** about its middle are summed over the nodes it shares.
- **The statics pin, per piece:** what its joints put on it, plus its own load and reactions, must sum to zero.
  Anything that passed through an unlabelled or overlapping voxel, or a solve that didn't converge, shows as a
  residual. Over 1% of the case's load, nothing of that case is read from the FEA.
  - **Measured:** 0.00000 on slice 1's stool (both cases) and on the box stool. On Theo's stools (step-stool-5, a-step-stool-so-my-6, every case): 0.00000.
  - **On the way, the pin caught my labelling:** 30% at first (the force at three-piece corners was lost), then 15%
    (a square leg read as round, with rounded ends reaching into the top, so its corners were no piece's).
- **Labelling:** each voxel goes to the nearest piece that can have it. A sheet can have a voxel within half a voxel of
  its solid. A stick is a flat-ended prism, and claims its corners (out to its half-diagonal) where no nearer piece can.
  A voxel surely inside two pieces is an overlap, no piece's; the pin reads it (pinned: aprons declared 6 mm into the
  top).
- **What it doesn't read** (said, and the hand rule's stands or the joint is not checked, never ✓):
  - a piece fewer than `VOXELS_THROUGH` voxels through;
  - fewer than 4 shared nodes;
  - a load or a support on the joint's own nodes;
  - a joint the hand check doesn't model as an edge on a face (a dado, a through tenon, a notch).

**3. Beside the hand rule** (`fea_joints.beside`, from `load_study.check_load`):
- The FEA bonds the pieces, so its moment is a fully fixed joint's, which errs safe for the joint.
  - **The moment to the fixings:** about the joint's line, over half the edge's thickness; across it, over half its
    length, or at the farthest fixing (M x / Σx²) where they're placed; turning in its plane, as shear the same way.
  - **The rows say** "the FEA holds the pieces as one, so its moment is a fully fixed joint's".
- **The waiver** (the foreman's (a) and (iii)): where a joint can't take pull, the bonded model's pull goes round it,
  said "the FEA bonds the X to the Y, a pull its fixing can't give; the load goes round it, as the hand check has it":
  - **a post:** only the moment's pull is waived; a net lift is ✗;
  - **an edge joint:** its direct net pull too, but never past a raked leg's thrust;
  - **where the hand rule has no number:** not checked, never ✓.
- **Raked legs** (the foreman's guard): H = R tan(rake) at each raked leg's top, by hand, on whatever ties it. It is
  checked always, ✗ at 0 pull: "the legs splay and the X is all that ties them (it leans 10°)".
- **An apron's end on a leg** (the foreman's (α)):
  - **Where the top bears on the apron** (contact): the FEA's shear at the apron's end stands: "the top bears on the
    front apron, and it hands 179 lb to the front right leg through one #8 screw…".
  - **Where the apron hangs from the top by an uncredited glue line:** that shear is waived as the bonded model's.
- **A disagreement of 25% or more** is said on the row.
- **A joint the FEA fails** says what would take it past its need: "3 more #8 screws at the front apron's end, or a
  glue block, would take it past 2", on its row and in the card's hedge. The card and the hedge say "by the FEA" where its number is
  the lower.
