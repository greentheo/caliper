### 2026-11-11 · CALI-75 (#42) · The coupon bench sheet, and beam theory against whittle

Theo's change to #42: calibrate against small coupons measured by hand. This is the first step, which needs no samples. `caliper/coupons.py` defines nine three-point-bend coupons (2x4 on edge and flat, 1/2 and 3/4 in ply along and across the grain, PETG printed flat, on edge and standing). It writes a one-page bench sheet (`pm/bench/coupon-sheet.html`) and a table of beam theory against whittle (`pm/bench/theory-vs-whittle.md`, raw numbers in the JSON beside it).

What the table found. The solver is close when the supports are out of the way: 0.87 to 0.96 of theory in sag at 6 voxels across. Held the way the check holds a part, a strip acts clamped at both ends: 0.12 to 0.21 of theory in sag and 1.6 to 3.2 times theory in break load at 4 across. A depth that isn't a whole number of voxels also rounds up to the next whole voxel, which adds stiffness on the coupons long enough for the check to choose a coarser size. A sheet resting on other pieces also gets the plate or beam check, so that verdict doesn't lean on this. A printed part held at two patches has only the FEA. No verdict changed: that is Theo's call, and the numbers are in the table.

PG-S (printed standing) isn't on the plan's list. It's the only coupon whose bending pulls the layers apart, which is what `LAYERS_ACROSS` and #64 item 6 need measured.
