### 2026-11-11 · CALI-162 (#240) · Sizes two parts share live in the plan, and fit is measured

On step-stool-4, the skeleton gave the stretcher's height but not its section. Both sides were written before the stretcher, and each guessed a 2×4 for its pocket (38.1 × 88.9 mm). The stretcher came out 3/4 in plywood 4 in tall (19 × 101.6 mm). The touch rules couldn't see it.

1. **The planner owns every size two parts share.** The plan prompt says that a number two parts must agree on lives in the skeleton, never in either part. Where one part sits in, on or through another, the joining sizes go in the skeleton, with a `fits_in` mate between the two: the inserted part first, its value the clearance a side.
2. **Parts read a shared size, never restate it.** `skeleton.shadows` fails a part's round (`skeleton_shadow`, one of the first build's repairs) in two cases:
   - a `PARAMS` key with a skeleton name and another value;
   - a near-name: the same subject plus a size word, like `stretcher_w` beside `stretcher_thickness`.

   It says "side_left sets its own stretcher thickness: 38.1 mm against the plan's 19 mm", and the designer's next round reads the plan's number. The same number in inches agrees. A part's own size that the plan doesn't share is its own.
3. **Fit is measured.** A `fits_in` mate is `fit_off(a, b, c) <= 0.5`.
   - `measure.pocket` finds the axis the part goes in along, the one it sticks out of the host on.
   - Rays from its centre line, half way in, give each side's gap to the host's wall. A negative gap is an overlap, and a side with no wall (an open slot) has none.
   - A fit passes within ±0.5 mm of the clearance a side (0.3 mm unless the plan says).
   - The result names both parts and the play: "the stretcher has 19.1 mm of play in side_left's pocket, and is 12.7 mm too big for it the other way" (Theo's v1, measured). A part that doesn't reach the pocket is an error, never a pass.
4. **Order.** A part a pocket is cut to, whose section the skeleton doesn't give, is built before the part with the pocket. Its two smaller sizes then go into the skeleton as `{part}_thickness` and `{part}_height`, source "measured", before the pocket's part is written. A section the plan gives keeps the plan's order. Building the inserted part first means its designer doesn't see the host yet. The skeleton's numbers place it.

**What this covers of #3c.** #3c's skeleton numbers driving the parts landed as `skeleton.drive`. Even `stretcher_h` against the plan's `stretcher_height` was false: the plan's is where the stretcher sits, and the part's is how tall the board is.
1. **Skeleton numbers say what they are.** Each has a `kind`: size, position, count or load. The planner sets it, and otherwise it's read from the unit and the why ("sits 3 in above the floor" is a position). `shadows` compares a part's number only with a plan size of the same subject and the same size word, with no closest-value fallback and never a position against a size. A part's own sizes (`top_t` on the top) are its own. A same-value restatement is a note ("restates the plan's ply thickness as ply_t"). So is another part's size the plan doesn't name ("the plan doesn't name the stretcher width: side_left sets it at 19 mm"), which is what really went wrong on step-stool-4. Pinned: its four parts as built give no false fail.
2. **`skeleton_shadow` stays a repair,** now that it only fails on a true mismatch.
3. **An overlap never passes a fit.** Any negative gap is past the band. The rays go at the entry, middle and end of the part's run inside the host, the tightest counting. Pinned: −0.2 mm a side, and a pocket narrowing to 18.6 mm at its inner face.
4. **A tilted part isn't measured:** "goes in at an angle: its fit isn't measured", an error and never a false "too big". An open slot says which sides it measured: "open on the top and bottom: measured on 2 sides".
5. **A section goes into the skeleton only for a board,** square to the axes and filling 90% of its box. Otherwise the build says it couldn't measure it and asks for the thickness and height. A measured size carries `kind: size` and `of: <part>`, and `write_part` measures it again whenever that part changes.
6. **Fit results say "on the drawn sizes".**

**Second rework (the re-check, the foreman's four).**
1. **Build-first fires on step-stool-4.** A part's section counts as known only when the plan names both its thickness and its height as sizes. Step-stool-4's `stretcher_height` is where it sits, so the stretcher is built before both sides. Its height is measured in as `stretcher_h`, since the plan's `stretcher_height` is taken by the position; that's the name the sides use, so it's compared by exact name. Only the size the plan doesn't name is measured.
2. **A part's own size is compared when the plan shares it.** The stretcher's `stretcher_t` 25 against the plan's `stretcher_thickness` 19 fails, and so does side_left's `side_t` against a plan `side_thickness`. A size of its own that the plan doesn't share is still its own.
3. **The size word decides first.** Thickness, width, depth, length, diameter, radius, spacing and clearance are always sizes. Only a height or an offset-like name (`_z`, `_rise`, `_offset`, `_elev`) goes on to the plan's `kind`, then its why. With neither, it's "unclear", and a mismatch on it is a note ("the plan's stretcher height may be where something sits: side_left sets 101.6 mm, the plan 76.2 mm"). Pinned: the reviewer's four whys, and step-stool-4 with every why emptied.
4. **A board revised into an angle** drops its measured section and says so in its history. **A part standing proud in an open slot** reads "doesn't sit in side_left's slot: it's 48.0 mm off its bottom".
