### 2026-11-11 · CALI-162 (#240) · Sizes two parts share live in the plan, and fit is measured

On step-stool-4, the skeleton gave the stretcher's height but not its section. Both sides were written before the stretcher, and each guessed a 2×4 for its pocket (38.1 × 88.9 mm). The stretcher came out 3/4 in plywood 4 in tall (19 × 101.6 mm). The touch rules couldn't see it.

1. **The planner owns every size two parts share.** The plan prompt says that a number two parts must agree on lives in the skeleton, never in either part. Where one part sits in, on or through another, the joining sizes go in the skeleton, with a `fits_in` mate between the two: the inserted part first, its value the clearance a side.
2. **Parts read a shared size, never restate it.** `skeleton.shadows` fails a part's round (`skeleton_shadow`, one of the first build's repairs) in two cases:
   - a `PARAMS` key with a skeleton name and another value;
   - a near-name: the same subject plus a size word, like `stretcher_w` beside `stretcher_thickness`.

   It says "side_left sets its own stretcher thickness: 38.1 mm against the plan's 19 mm", and the designer's next round reads the plan's number. The same number in inches agrees. A part's own size that the plan doesn't share is its own.
3. **Fit is measured.** A `fits_in` mate is `fit_off(a, b, c) <= 0.5`.
   - `measure.pocket` finds the axis the part goes in along, the one it sticks out of the host on.
   - Rays from its centre line, half way in, give each side's gap to the host's wall. A negative gap is an overlap, and a side with no wall (an open slot) has none.
   - A fit passes within ±0.5 mm of the clearance a side (0.3 mm unless the plan says).
   - The result names both parts and the play: "the stretcher has 19.1 mm of play in side_left's pocket, and is 12.7 mm too big for it the other way" (Theo's v1, measured). A part that doesn't reach the pocket is an error, never a pass.
4. **Order.** A part a pocket is cut to, whose section the skeleton doesn't give, is built before the part with the pocket. Its two smaller sizes then go into the skeleton as `{part}_thickness` and `{part}_height`, source "measured", before the pocket's part is written. A section the plan gives keeps the plan's order. Building the inserted part first means its designer doesn't see the host yet. The skeleton's numbers place it.

**What this covers of #3c.** #3c's skeleton numbers driving the parts landed as `skeleton.drive`. This adds the other half of #3's point 4: a part that hard-codes a shared number instead of reading it fails a check, which is the shadow check, plus the measured fit the touch rules couldn't give.
