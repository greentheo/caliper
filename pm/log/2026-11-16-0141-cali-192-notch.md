### 2026-11-16 · CALI-192 (#310) · A notch joint by hand: bearing on its closed faces, nothing for a pull along it, and the side's net section past it

#311 named v7's bottom stringer joints without a number: the stringer runs through notches cut up from each side's bottom edge, out to their outer faces, not edge on face. This is their rule, with the foreman's two calls on #310.

- **Found as a notch** (`_notch`): a piece that runs through another's thickness where it crosses that piece's plane, inside its outline. That covers a notch already cut in the outline (v7's), and a through tenon or notch the through-finder reports.
  - **The geometry:** where it crosses is a rectangle, its line there by its thickness.
  - **Its faces:** each face is closed where the host has wood just past it, and open where it doesn't. v7's notch is closed on its sides and top, open at the bottom.
- **What holds it** (`notch_row`):
  - **Toward a closed face,** it bears, at #301's 360 psi on the face's length × the host's thickness.
  - **Toward an open face,** it lifts out: "the stringer lifts out of the left side's notch: nothing holds it".
  - **Along its own length,** nothing in a notch holds a pull: "the stringer's notch can't hold a pull along it: glue to the left side's edge grain, no credit". The foreman's call: only a declared screw through the side's face into its end, a pin, a wedge or a glue block would. Screws into end grain are no credit too (NDS §12.2.2.3).
- **Routing** reads a notch's own faces: a push toward a closed face bears there; one toward an open face goes to a joint that bears it.
- **The side's net section past its notch** (`net_row`, the foreman's call: by hand, not left to the FEA):
  - **The number:** the vertical load the side carries, over the width left just under the notch's top × its thickness, against the strength the FEA judges it by, taken the weaker way for plywood (`across`), as the plate check does.
  - **Said as** "net section, no allowance for the stress at the notch's corner".
  - **The material** is the run's own, passed from the check.
- **A splayed part tied through notches** now reads its tie's pull, ✗, named: a notched stringer can't hold the feet together. A tie the check still can't place (set into a dado) is named by its own reason: "its tie, the stringer, isn't checked by hand: set into the other (a dado or rabbet)".
- **v7 (scratch copy):** the stringer's two joints are placed and carry none of the three loads; nothing is left "not checked by hand". Each side's net section past its notch reads 118× (500 lb cases) and 179× (its rating). The weakest joint is still the front rail's, 3.28×.
