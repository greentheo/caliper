### 2026-11-20 · CALI-203 (#335) · round three: a thinned wall's buckling goes as t³

caliper-reviewer on #354 at 6d3361a: `_thinner` scaled a thinned wall's buckling by ratio². But `buckling.check`'s
safety is σ_cr·b·t / push, with σ_cr ∝ (t/b)², so it goes as t³. On the all-XPS-walls box, rebuilt and checked at each
thickness:

| walls | rebuilt check | the row at t² | at t³ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 in | 16.88 | | |
| 1½ in | 6.76 | 9.49 | 7.12 |
| 1 in | ✗ 1.89 | ✓ 4.22 | 2.11 |

**The fix:**
- **A thinned wall's buckling** goes as ratio³.
- **The generic branch:** a case whose check can't name a piece (old runs' buckling, a holed plate, a joint) goes as
  ratio³ too, the steepest, on the safe side. It was ratio² on main.

**Every other thickness power, checked:**
- **The plate fallback** (`safety × ratio²` where the plate can't be re-read): a plate's bending stress goes as t², so
  that's right.
- **The plate check's own formulas** (`load_study`): stress as t², deflection as t³. Exact, so they're right.
- **`fixes.py`'s "thicker sheet" for a wall that buckles:** already t³ (`_thicker(…, 3.0)`).
- **The FEA's number in `_thinner`:** scaled by ratio¹. That's right for a wall in compression. For a panel in bending,
  stress goes as t², so ratio¹ reads high. The reviewer measured it conservative on this box (the FEA rose from 111 to
  122 as the walls thinned), but the FEA's worst can be anywhere. I haven't changed it: it would move Theo's
  step-stool-5 thinner rows. It goes to the foreman.

**Pinned**, on the synthetic two-stock part, where t² and t³ differ:
- a thinned wall at 16.88 reads 7.12 at 1½ in and 2.11 at 1 in (t² said 9.49 and 4.22);
- a joint-governed case at 8.0 reads 3.37 at 1½ in (t² said 4.5).

The other-stock pin keeps its own claim, the floor's 2.5 never thinned.

**Fail first:** both new pins failed at 6d3361a. **Mutants:** each branch back at ratio², each caught by its own pin.
