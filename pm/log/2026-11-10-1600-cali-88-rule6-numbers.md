### 2026-11-10 · CALI-88 (#64) · The numbers a person reads are never greener than the check

Rule 6, on the numbers. This is the first of #64's three PRs and covers items 1, 2, 9, 13, 15 and 16. Item 6 waits for #42's PETG coupons, item 5 gets its own PR, and the wording items get another.

1. **A fail never reads as its limit.** A safety of 1.96 against a limit of 2 used to read "only 2 times over, and it needs 2", with a fail badge of "doesn't hold 2.0x". Within 10% of its limit, either way, a safety is now said to two places, cut rather than rounded: "1.96 times over". A fail further under is cut to one place, so 1.67 reads 1.6, not 1.7. The fail badge is cut to one place as well. Nothing under 10.0 reads "more than 10": 9.96 is "9.9 times over".
2. **Amber.** A pass at or under 1.25 times its limit is `close`. It holds, and it still counts as a pass, so nothing it unlocks gets locked. Its badge is amber, and its line adds "close to the 2 it needs". The band is the four-questions spec's proposal, and the owner may change it (`verdicts.CLOSE`). A tipping case's factor stays out of the band. The scorecard counts it as a pass. Every reader that listed "holds" and "fails" now uses `verdicts.SETTLED` or `verdicts.PASSES`, so a close pass shows its number in the kids' book, the build book, the plate panel and the scorecard.
9. **One number, against one thing.** The foreman picked option B. "N times over" stays the safety against the load times its factor, as the cards always said it, so no verdict moves. Where the factor isn't 1, the line says what that is: "3.1 times over 400 lb (200 lb, twice over)". That way a reader who multiplies 3.1 by Dad's 200 lb isn't misled about where it breaks. The card, the kids' book, the trial panel and the build book say the same number, and a test pins it on the sample stool. Saying it against the load itself (6.2 here) is Theo's to switch to.
13. **A failed test's line.** It used to say "We predicted it would hold 400", but 400 is what it must hold, not the prediction. It now says both numbers: "It had to hold 400 (200 lb, twice over for …), and we predicted about 1,240." The prediction is the number #42's calibration needs.
15. **The design card's strength line.** It used to read "✓ Holds Dad (200 lb), 2 times over (assumed): 3.1 times over". It now reads "✓ Holds Dad (200 lb), 3.1 times over (it needs 2, assumed)".
16. **Chat replies.** The picture and carve replies say their strength number as the cards do, and a reply that says it holds ends with a pointer to the promise. Lighten and refine have no chat reply with a strength number on main.

The whole suite is CI's.
