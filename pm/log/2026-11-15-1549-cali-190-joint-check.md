### 2026-11-15 · CALI-190 (#301) · A hand check of each joint, needing what the wood needs (#222 slice B)

Theo, 2026-11-14: "let's do the joints as the same safety factor that the rest of the stool is checked at (just for simplicity)" and "I like the hand check of each joint. So, yes".

**The capacities, checked against their sources first.** The table is posted on #301 (issuecomment-6900765231). A research pass and I read:
- NDS 2018 and the NDS 2005 Commentary;
- the Wood Handbook (2021);
- APA's PDS (2020), its Plywood Design Specification Y510 (1997), and Technical Note E830 (screws in plywood).

I re-checked E830 Table 6, Y510 Table 3 and the PDS bearing value in the downloaded text myself. Five values were the foreman's to decide, and he took the safe side; each is one constant with its source line, marked `pending: Theo`, and the PR waits on Theo's answer:
1. **A screw in shear into an edge:** E830's 180 lb (a #8 into a 3/4 in edge, 1 in of thread), scaled by the thread the bought screw has there, never past 1 in. The shopping list now buys #8 × 2 in for 3/4 in plywood (1 1/4 in into the edge, `fasteners.EDGE_THREAD_IN`). Before, it bought 1 1/4 in, which put half an inch into an edge, 90 lb. The designer's rule says the same (part_scripts.md).
2. **Withdrawal from an edge:** no credit. NDS §12.2.2.3 says so; E830 tested 360 lb but advises against relying on it.
3. **Face glue:** 53 psi, an allowable and not a mean, the safe side (40 psi for a 3-ply panel). It's for the face-glued joints when their rule is added.
4. **Bearing:** 360 psi, the PDS design value.
5. **Load share:** where a piece rests on more than two supports, half each way, each pair by the lever rule.

Pocket screws, dowels and printed connectors stay "not checked": no source.

**The check** (`joint_check.py`):
- **Where:** whose edge meets whose face, read the way the patterns' screw marks read it (`marks._edge_on`). The screws it counts are the ones the list buys.
- **What each joint carries:** each load case × its factor goes from the piece it lands on, down its joints, to what holds the part, by the lever rule.
- **Mode:** from the force's direction: bearing, withdrawal or shear.
- **Capacity:** the lowest the fix gives.
- **Not a simple chain:** a path it can't follow says "its load path isn't a simple chain", never a ✓ or a ✗.
- **Governs where it's lowest,** through the path buckling's number takes:
  - `check_load`, plus `judged_by`, which treats a joint as a hand check, so a pass goes back to what it took over from;
  - `governing_words` ("the joint between the top and the left");
  - Lighten's `judged` and `judge_holed`;
  - `fail_hedge`: "The joint between the top and the left gives first: two #8 screws in shear into the top's edge, 0.9x of what it needs";
  - the card: "Its joints, by hand: the weakest, between …";
  - the page: the case line's "(by …)", and a "Joints, by hand" table in each case.
- **Its safety is cut toward safe,** and each row carries the server's own verdict (the guards).
- **The "joints not checked" note stays only for joints it couldn't check:** the badge says how many, and the line beside it names them.
