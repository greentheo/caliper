### 2026-11-12 · CALI-156 (#222) slice A · Caliper reads how a part's pieces join, and says the check takes them as solid

#222's first slice, on Theo's answer to question 1 ("✓ with a note for joints"), with the through-tenon case from
step-stool-5 and #272's review note. No capacity numbers yet (that's slice B).

- **`JOINTS`, a module-level list in the part's script, read from its text and never run (`joints.read`).** It's data,
  like `HOLES`: two pieces, a kind, and what holds it. An entry that isn't plain data isn't read.
- **Every contact is a joint (`joints.contacts`):**
  - an edge on a face, as `fasteners.joints` found them;
  - a sheet run through another (a through tenon) or set into it (a dado or rabbet), found from the pieces' own
    planes. A piece is "in" a host only where it reaches more than its own thickness in from the host's edge. So a
    stringer's end flush in a side is in the side, not the side in the stringer, and a dado running out at the side's
    edges still counts.
  - `fasteners.joints` now reads these contacts, so the shopping list has glue and screws where a stringer passes
    through. #272's review found it had none: the edge probes miss a stringer standing proud of the sides.
- **Checked in each authoring round (`joints.check`):**
  - `joints`: each entry's pieces exist and touch, and its kind and fix are ones Caliper knows. Each failure is said:
    "JOINTS names the shelf, which isn't one of its pieces"; "… joins the step and the bottom stringer, which don't
    touch"; "… the kind "weld", which isn't one of butt, bevel, …".
  - `mortises`: a piece through another's outline has its mortise cut there: "the bottom stringer passes through the
    left side, but the left side's outline has no hole where it goes: cut the mortise into the left side's outline". A
    hole elsewhere in the side isn't the mortise. A piece set in (a dado) wants none.
  - The designer's round fails on it and is told where to cut.
- **Listed (`joints.listed`):** its `JOINTS` entries, then every other contact as an assumed butt (or through tenon),
  glue and screws. Built-from shows "How it joins", and the book has its own "How it joins" table. Each says an
  assumed one is assumed, and that the strength check takes them as solid wood.
- **The verdict says it, through #265's path.** A strength run of a part with more than one wood piece records its joint
  count (`wood_joints`), and a pass on it carries the joints flag at `JOINT_CAP` ("✓ with a note"). The card says "Its
  joints aren't checked: the check takes its 4 joints as solid wood." The page's case card says the same. The card's goal
  line, the scorecard and the study's headline read the same flag (#265's path). Two gaps:
  - a Lighten step doesn't carry the count yet, since it's judged against the Lighten run, which has none;
  - a strength run made before this has no count, so it says nothing until the part is checked again.

**Rework from the review (the foreman's four).**
1. **A through tenon reads one way.** The mortise belongs in the host only where the host's outline goes round the
   crossing. Where the crossing reaches the host's edge, the host is the tenon. Pinned on a stringer standing 1 in proud
   of each side: a mortise in each side, nothing in the stringer. (It had also told the designer to hole the stringer.)
2. **A `JOINTS` that isn't plain data fails the round in words:** "JOINTS isn't plain data: write it as a list of dicts".
   It's never dropped. The annotated form (`JOINTS: list = [...]`) is read. A comprehension or a loop's appends is said;
   both are pinned. When it can't be read, a contact is never listed as unsaid: it's marked unread. An unlisted contact
   that's set in is an assumed dado, not a butt.
3. **No bare ✓ on a multi-piece wood part.** A run read back from before slice A, a run whose count failed, and a Lighten
   step (from the run it comes from) all read "✓ … the check takes its joints as solid wood", with no count. This works
   by marking a run `wood_joints: "uncounted"` where its part's script declares more than one wood piece (`joints.wooden`,
   from the text).
4. **One contact finder.** `contacts()` gives each pair where on each sheet the other meets it (`on`, in that sheet's
   (u, v)). Its reach is a parameter (the tolerance by default), so Lighten's rim can switch to it, with its own reach,
   once #272 and this have both landed.

and its test is tightened.
