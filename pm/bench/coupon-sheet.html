Coupon bend test

A strip on two supports, loaded in the middle. Caliper will compare what you read here with what it predicted for the same strip (issue #42).

Each coupon

  1. Measure its width and depth at mid-span, and the span. Weigh it if you can.
  2. Rest it on two round supports, 1 in pipe or dowel, on sawhorses or a bench. Don't fix it down: the ends must be free to turn and slide. Mark the middle.
  3. Hang the load from a strap over a 1 in wood saddle at the middle. Keep the load two inches off the floor.
  4. Hang the empty bucket or hanger, about 5 lb. Zero the reading there. That load doesn't count: write loads above it.
  5. Go to each of the three load steps. Wait 30 seconds and read the sag. The steps are targets: write what you actually hung.
  6. Back off to the empty hanger. The reading should come back near zero. If it doesn't, something slipped or crushed: redo it.
  7. Where the table says to break it, add load in small steps until it goes. Write the most it held, and how it broke.
Safety. Eye protection. Nobody stands on a coupon or under the load, and feet stay clear of where it lands. Wood can snap hard and send splinters, so stand to the side when you get near the break.

Loading: use what you have

  • Sand or gravel in buckets, weighed before you hang them. Two part-full buckets are safer than one full one.
  • Bathroom scale: weigh each bucket on it, or hold the bucket and subtract your own weight. Or set one support on the scale, zero it with the coupon resting, and double what it reads as you load.
  • Weights or bagged concrete on a hanger board.
  • Hanging scale and ratchet strap to a floor anchor: the scale reads the load directly, and the load eases off when the coupon breaks.

Reading the sag: use what you have

  • Dial indicator on a stand on the floor, tip against the underside beside the strap. Best for anything that sags under 3 mm.
  • Rule and thread: stretch a thread tight between two pins in the coupon, over the supports. Tape a steel rule upright at mid-span, and read it where the thread crosses, at eye level, to 1/64 in or 0.5 mm. The thread moves with the coupon at the supports, so crushed supports don't count.

Sag and break are predictions from the textbook values Caliper uses today, at its default print settings (1.2 mm walls, 20% infill). Real wood often beats them by a lot, and finding out by how much is the point. Three of each kind beats one. PETG may fold instead of snapping: write the most it held. Printed: note layer height, walls, infill and pattern, filament, nozzle temperature. Wood: note knots near the middle, and moisture if you have a meter.
